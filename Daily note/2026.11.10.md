## **💡 Insight**
**- Algebra: Into to rings**
- Intuitionally, a ring is a *nice* **binary oporation** pushed into an **abelian group**. There word here 'nice', more concretly means
	1. Close and associative in R
	2. Distributes over +.
- So formally $(R, +, *)$ is a ring if 
	- $(R,+)$ is an abelian group
	- $*$ is a *binary oporation* that is **closed** and **associative** on $R$
	- For all $x, y, z\in R$ we have $x*(y+z) = x*y + x*z$ and $(x+y)*z = x*z + y*z$
- Also intuitionally, a ring has (way) more structure then a group
- **unity** of a ring is $1\in R$ such that $\forall a \in R$ we have $a*1 = 1* a = a$. For $0 \in (R,+)$ being the *identity*, we can prove that $a* 0  = 0*a = 0$. This means that $1$ **preserves under multiplication** and $0$ **infects** (Very intuitially speaking). 

- **Probability**
- The proof that $\Omega$ is finite and all data point $\omega$ has equal probability, the $\mathbb{P}(A) = \frac{A}{\Omega}$. The outline is we first consider about singletons, and then break $A$ down into singletons.
- One possible method is to use $P(A) \geq 0$ (Axiom 1) as a bounding argument. In most cases, this may be two loose, to **strength the argument**, we can **pick thing out of $A$ .** i.e Choose an *appropiate set* $E$, such that we break $A = (A \cap E)\bigsqcup   (A-E)$, and use *axiom 3(finite ver)* of probability measure axiom to break the **disjoint union** apart, and make one of them $0$ as a bound. This gives us **the freedom to choose E**, and hence a way to **control the tightness of the bound**
