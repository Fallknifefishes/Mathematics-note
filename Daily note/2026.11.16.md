## **💡 Insight**
**Analysis**
- Indexing: For a subsequence of $(x_{n})$ (namely $(x_{n_{k}})$), the **index** $n_{k}$ actually has two meanings. One is the $n_{k}'th$ term in the original sequence, the other is the $k'th$ term is the *subsequence*. We always have $n_{k}\ge k$, which connects index of the original sequence ($n_{k}$) to the index of the subsequence $k$
- Alternate proof of *Bolzano Weierstrass*. Lemma: For any real sequence $(x_{n})$, we can find **either** a **monotone increasing** or a **monotone decreasing sequence**. The **idea** of the proof is to define a 'peak' element which **controlls the size of infinite terms behind it**. 
- So for a sequence $(x_{n})$, we call $x_{m}$ a *peak* if for all $k\ge m$, $x_{k} \le x_{m}$ (This controlls **infinite behaviour**). If there are *infinite peak terms*, then these terms form a *decreasing sequence*. Otherwise, if there are **finite peak**, for sufficiently large enough $x_k$ (*looking far enough such that there are no more peaks*), we can pick $x_{k+n} > x_{k}$, and repeat. Forcing an **increasing subsequence**. Then use monotone convergence.
- The **idea** of this proof is to use the **definition of** *peak* to control the size of infinite terms.

- **Idea 1** For series limit, one can reduce thing into *sequence*, use a *general finite arguement*, push to $\infty$ and convert back to series
- **Idea 2** Finite quantity does not effect infinite behaviours. 
	- Eg Suppose $C>0$ is a constant, $(a_{k}), (b_{k})$ are sequences and after large enough $N$ we have $0\le a_{k}\le Cb_{k}$. The if $\sum b_{k}$ converges,  $\sum a_{k}$ also converge.
	- This generalise two things from the **original comparison test.** One is the **scaling** and the other is the **eventual order relationship**. Because these are finite properties, they will not effect infinite behaviour (such as convergence).
- **Dicotomy of series convergence:** If we have $(a_n)$ for which $a_{n}>0$ $\forall n\in \mathbb{N}$ then $\sum a_{n}$ **either** converges or diverges to $\infty$. This is due to **an increasing sequence** is **either** *bounded or unbounded*.
