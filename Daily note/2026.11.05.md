## **💡 Insight**
- For a hom mapping $\phi:G\to H$ , if $\ker(\phi) = \{e_{G}\}$ and $\mathrm{Im}(\phi) = H$ then $\phi$ is isomorphic
- The idea is that, for a hom mapping, **image set** measure how *surjective* a mapping is and the **kernal** measures of *injective* in mapping is. For example, if $a\neq e$ is in the kernal, then for every $b$ we have $\phi(b)=\phi(a.b)$ (ie if we *put $a$  in the kernal*, the mapping becames **less injective**)

 - Today, I read about the concept of *normal subgroups*. 
 - If $H\leq G$ is a subgroup of G, if $\forall x\in G$, $x^{-1}Hx \subset H$ the we say $H$ is a *normal subgroup* of $G$, denoted as $H \trianglelefteq G$
 - The the following are equivalent
	 - $H\trianglelefteq G$
	 - $\forall x \in G$, $xH = Hx$
	 - $\forall x \in G$, $x^-1 H x = H$
- The idea of proving this is to *exploit* conditions as a whole to control individual. (i.e zoom out and find properties, and zoom in to use the properties)

- The way I intuitionally thing about it is, we are studying **communitativity of objects with a certain structure** (i.e subgroups). This is indeed *more general*  then the communitativity of **individual elements**.
- The other way to view it is seeing the left/right multiplication of $x$ as a mapping from $H$, and they have the same **image set** for *every $x$.*

- Once we have **normal subsets**, the *left* and *right* cosets make no distinction, so it makes sense if we only talk about left for the moment. If $H \trianglelefteq G$, then $\forall x, y \in G$ we have $xH yH = xyH$.
- So if we consider $G/H$ be the **set of left cosets of H in G**, we see that $(G/H, *)$ is a group where $*: G/H \to G/H$, $xH * yH = xHyH =^{(1)} xyH$. Where $(1)$ is because $H$ is a normal subset.

- **Question** what if $H$ is not a normal subgroup, will such quotient sets still exist? will such quotient group still exist? If not, which part of the existance of such group reply on *normal subgroup properties*.
