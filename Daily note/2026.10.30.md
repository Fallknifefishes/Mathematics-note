**Understanding of the stuctures of subgroups**
It if closed, meaning 
1. inside cannot get out $\forall a,b \in G$, $a.b\in G$
2. Outside cannot get in $\forall g \in G$, $b \not\in G$ $g.b\notin G$. So $\{gb|g\in G\}$ creates a structure that is **similar** but **distinct** from $G$. If we look at $G$ under a larger structure (i.e subgroup), this inspires the idea of **cosets**
3. However, when $x, y\not\in G$ and $x.y\in G$, then is reduces to 2. with $g = x.y$ and $b = y^{-1}$

Give that $.$ is associative
$G$ is a group $\iff$  $\forall g \in G$, $Gg = G = gG$

$G$ is a finite group $\iff$  $\forall g \in G$ ,  $Gg$ = $G$ = $gG$ $\iff$ $.$ is closed, and **left and right cancellation** 
So essentially **left and right cancellation** gives **injectivity**, we need the *finite* condition to force surjectivity. Whereas $Gg = G$ gives **surjectivity and closure all at once**.

The proof of $G$ is a group $\leftarrow$  $\forall g \in G$, $Gg = G = gG$. The idea if 
1. Show that each element has a right identity. $\forall x\in G, \exists e_{x}$
2. Show that arbitrary two element has the same right identity $e_x = e$

3. So $\exists e, \forall x$. There exists a right identity for the group

4. Similarly, there exists an left identity for the group. We then show they are equal
