## **💡 Insight**
- Algebra: Alternating groups. The idea is *a permutation of A* can be written as composition of *transpositions* between two elements. For $|A| = n$, (we can just study 1....n, but for the sake of completeness, let $A= \{x_{1}, x_{2},\dots,x_{n}\}$) we can define the quantity $P_{A}=\prod_{1\le i < j \le n}(x_{i} - x_{j})$ (With the aim of finding inveriant). And due to symmetry, if we perform a transposition of two elements on $A$ to form $A'$, we get $P_{A'} = -P_{A}$
- So essentially, every element in $S_{n}$ is either *even* or *odd* number of composition of transpositions (Which is measured by computing $P_{\alpha({A})}$ and see if the sign differs for $P_A$) 
- Those $\alpha \in S_{n}$ that it's *alternating polynomial* $P_{\alpha(A)}$ does not differ (i.e even number of transpositions) from $P_A$ forms a group, and a subgroup of $S_n$ , this is the alternating group

- Physics; Works done defined as the summation of a force over a path given a definition; given the assumption path does not matter (i.e the same amount of work is done travelling to the destination for any path)
- This force (which give the assumption) a *conservative force*. **conservative force does no work** (over a closed loop).
- Assumption: Here we assume, for a given destation, a **conservative force exists, and give unique value of the work done**. Hence we can define work done.
