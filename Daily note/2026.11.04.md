## **💡 Insight**
- If we want to prove $(a_{n}) \to l$, we can construct $h_{n} = a_{n} -l$ and by bounding, show that $h_{n} \to {0}$

- Direct product. If $(G, .)$ and $(H, *)$ are groups, then $(G\times H, @)$ is also group, where $(g_{1},h_{1}) @ (g_{2}, h_{2}) = (g_{1}g_{2}, h_{1}h_{2})$
- Intuitionall, this is combining information of two group structure to **a single tuple**, and so that we can see there behaviour at once. Nothing essential about the groups are changes, they are now inside their position of the tuple.
- We can generalise this into $N$ groups, as **this in essence is just joining group together**. And see how they **interact with eachother**.
